CSU researchers will lead a project to develop a first-of-its-kind data-driven AI model to improve understanding of ice-containing clouds and precipitation. CSU expertise will also contribute to a second project focused on optimizing remote power grids. The Genesis Mission is a historic national initiative led by the U.S. Department of Energy, which is building the world’s most powerful integrated science discovery platform. By uniting government, industry, academia and philanthropy, it is accelerating breakthroughs in energy, scientific discovery and national security through a new platform that combines AI, supercomputing, quantum systems and advanced scientific instruments. “These projects represent the kind of bold, collaborative science needed to accelerate discovery on our most complex challenges,” said Cassandra Moseley, CSU vice president for research. “They bring together CSU’s deep expertise in atmospheric science, power grids, advanced observations and artificial intelligence to create new capabilities that could benefit researchers well beyond this individual effort.” The goal of the Phase I RFA awards is to identify promising pathways toward transformative scientific capabilities and establish a foundation for future investment and scale. Project teams will design and demonstrate research workflows that integrate AI with scientific investigation, while rigorously evaluating whether those approaches can accelerate discovery, improve predictive capabilities, enhance experimentation or generate new scientific insights. “These awards reflect CSU’s growing leadership at the intersection of artificial intelligence and atmospheric science,” CSU President Amy Parsons said. “Our researchers are using emerging technologies to tackle questions with enormous implications for communities, natural resources and our shared future. We are proud to contribute CSU’s expertise to this ambitious national initiative.” CSU’s project, “Latent Representation Learning for Understanding Ice Microphysical and Precipitation Processes Constrained by Radar and In Situ Observations,” will be led by principal investigator Christine Chiu, a professor in CSU’s department of Atmospheric Science, a department leading worldwide in the use of AI for atmospheric research and weather forecasting. Haonan Chen, an associate professor in Electrical and Computer Engineering, will serve as co-principal investigator. Ice-containing clouds play a central role in Earth’s energy balance and water cycle, generating a substantial share of global rain events from frozen precipitation particles that melt before reaching the surface. Yet these clouds remain among the least understood atmospheric systems because ice crystal growth depends on both local conditions and a particle’s growth history – information that observations rarely capture directly. Existing observations also sample only portions of clouds, providing limited connections between cloud processes aloft and the precipitation reaching the surface. The CSU-led team will approach this challenge by starting with surface precipitation and working backward to identify the minimum information needed to constrain precipitation formation. “This project will provide the scientific community with a fundamentally new way to understand how ice-containing clouds produce precipitation,” Chiu said. “By developing a flexible, data-driven foundation model, we aim to help accelerate discovery across atmospheric science and support future research on water resources, weather prediction and energy systems.” The team will combine recent advances in AI with comprehensive observations from the Department of Energy’s Atmospheric Radiation Measurement user facility. AI can extract compact, physically meaningful representations from high-dimensional radar and direct observations that cannot be fully uncovered through traditional analysis methods. These interpretable representations will offer a new way to connect cloud structure with microphysical states and the larger science community. The project’s primary deliverable will be a first-of-its-kind foundation model driven by Atmospheric Radiation Measurement data. The model will provide reusable representations for a broad range of atmospheric science applications without requiring researchers to retrain its core. The framework can be expanded over time by incorporating measurements from additional instruments into a unified system. This flexible approach will strengthen scientific capabilities, support energy and water resources applications, and make Atmospheric Radiation Measurement datasets more accessible and useful for AI-driven research. CSU also will contribute to AURORA-AI (Alaska Utility Resilience and Optimization using Real-time AI), a project led by University of Alaska Fairbanks focused on remote island microgrids serving rural and coastal communities in Alaska and other isolated regions. Zongjie Wang (left) at the Powerhouse Energy Institute. January 26, 2026. Two CSU award recipients will lead CSU’s contribution to the project: Zongjie Wang, director of the Grid Modernization Initiative at CSU’s Energy Institute , assistant professor of Electrical and Computer Engineering, and affiliated faculty member in systems engineering; and Tim Hansen, an associate professor of Electrical and Computer Engineering. Remote microgrids face high energy costs, limited and highly variable generation resources, and rapidly changing industrial demand. AURORA-AI will combine high-resolution utility data, physics-based digital twins and machine learning to forecast demand and generation; detect abnormal conditions; optimize the use of hydro, diesel, battery and flexible loads; and provide real-time recommendations to grid operators. CSU will lead the development of physics-informed forecasting, anomaly-detection and multi-agent reinforcement-learning tools that connect real-world utility data with a digital twin of Cordova’s islanded microgrid. “Through CSU’s Grid Modernization Initiative, we are advancing state-of-the-art AI technologies that strengthen grid reliability, resilience and real-time operations nationwide,” Wang said. “Projects such as AURORA-AI demonstrate how the Grid Modernization Initiative is helping lead national efforts to connect university research, national laboratories and utilities and translate advanced technologies into practical grid solutions. These capabilities will give operators faster, more accurate, and physically consistent decision support for coordinating hydropower, diesel generation, battery storage and rapidly changing data-center demand.” The project aims to improve reliability, lower operating costs, expand the use of distributed energy resources and create a scalable approach to AI-enabled controls for remote microgrids across the United States. “By bringing CSU and Colorado’s expertise in AI-enabled grid optimization to Alaska, we aim to create solutions that benefit remote communities and can be deployed across other islanded and constrained grids, including those in Puerto Rico and Hawaii,” Hansen said. “Much of today’s discussion focuses on the strain large data centers can place on power systems, yet a right-sized edge data center can become a valuable flexible load for an isolated microgrid with excess hydropower.” Related Stories CSU Energy Institute Department of Atmospheric Science Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering Research Walter Scott Jr College of Engineering Tiana Kennedy --> More posts by Tiana Kennedy Tag Cloud Alumni Athletics College of Agricultural Sciences College of Business College of Health and Human Sciences College of Liberal Arts College of Natural Sciences College of Veterinary Medicine and Biomedical Sciences covid-19 CSU Extension CSU Libraries CSU Online CSU Spur CSU System Department of Atmospheric Science Department of Biology Department of Food Science and Human Nutrition Department of Health and Exercise Science Department of Human Development and Family Studies Division of Student Affairs Facilities Management Graduate School High-Impact Practices Housing and Dining Services In memory International Programs James L.
