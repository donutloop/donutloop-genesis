The DOE Office of Science (SC), Office of Critical Minerals and Energy Innovation (CMEI), Office of Environmental Management (EM), Office of Nuclear Energy (NE), Office of Electricity (OE), and Hydrocarbonsand Geothermal Office (HGEO) hereby announce interest in receiving applications from interdisciplinary teams addressing the Genesis Mission National Science and Technology Challenges to accelerate scientific discovery and research and development (R&D) workflows using novel artificial intelligence (AI) models and frameworks. By achieving AI advantage, these teams will advance the DOE's mission and ensure America’s security and prosperity by addressing energy, environmental, and nuclear challenges through science and technology. Teams are encouraged to leverage the extensive scientific and data resources of the DOE/National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA), the National Laboratories, U.S. industry, and academia. The resulting AI models and workflows, if successful, may be integrated into the American Science Cloud. The Genesis Mission, as described in Executive Order 14363 . Additional information on relevant Genesis Mission activities is included in DOE’s December 10, 2025 AI for Science announcement. The projects must clearly articulate a vision for the science and/or applied energy initiatives that will be pursued and a description of how and why AI will enhance the scientific and technical workflows. It must clearly address the chosen focus area and explain how it advances or solves the corresponding challenge. A core philosophy of the Genesis Mission is that the whole is more than the sum of the parts, and specifically, that both specific developments (software, data, models, etc.) and best practices can be shared across the initiative. Accordingly, each team is expected to participate in coordination activities across the Genesis Mission for data, models, and infrastructure, and will be expected to provide information on project requirements, needs, and best practices as requested by the Genesis Mission. Phased Program Structure Phase I April 28, 2026: Phase I applications deadline December 17, 2026: Phase II applications resulting from Phase I awards Funding Range: $500,000–$750,000 over 9 months. Applicants for Phase I awards should prepare budgets with asingle period that starts on July 1, 2026 and ends on March 31, 2027. Focus Areas: Phase I applications are limited to a single focus area. Phase I projects are expected to provide a quantitative assessment of whether the proposed approach to demonstrating AI advantage in the scientific and/or technical research workflow is on a trajectory to yield a transformative scientific capability or transformative approach to an energy application, thereby justifying consideration for increased investment through a Phase II project. At the end of 6-months, Phase I teams will undergo a go/no go review. Phase I teams have the option to request up to a 3-months extension to their go/no go review date to complete planned tasks, such as experiments. Only a small fraction of Phase I teams will be selected for Phase II awards. These larger team partnerships (approximately 3 to 5 times the level of effort of Phase I awards per year for three years) may include multiple Phase I teams, removal of Phase I team members, or additional external researchers. Phase I teams that are not selected will end after 9-months. Teaming Arrangements Small teams attack a particular challenge focus area or part of a focus area. All teams in Phase I must include institutions from at least two of the following categories: (1) DOE/NNSA National Laboratory, (2) Industry,(3) IHE/Non-profit/Other. Phase II April 28, 2026: Phase II Letters of Intent Deadline (required) May 19, 2026: Phase II applications deadline Funding Range: Anticipated funding at 3–5 times the Phase I award, with a 3-year project duration. Focus Areas: Phase II applications must identify a primary focus area but can also address secondary focus areas. During the second phase, meritorious Phase I and new Phase II teams will pursue the promising directions identified during the first phase. Receipt of a Phase I award will not be a prerequisite for submitting a letter of interest and application for Phase II. Teams that believe they have already met the Phase I goals may apply directly for a Phase II award instead of a Phase I award. However, it is anticipated that most FY26 awards will be Phase I. Teaming Arrangements All teams in Phase II must include at least one institution from the following categories: (1) DOE/NNSA National Laboratory, (2) Industry, (3) IHE/Non-profit/Other are strongly encouraged but not required. Challenge Areas and Focus Areas Each applicant must address a topic and focus area given below. The challenge topics are a subset of the Genesis Mission National Science and Technology Challenges (Topics 1-17) and cross-cutting needs of the Genesis Mission (Topics 18-21). Applicant institutions are limited to no more than one application as the lead institution per focus area for Phase I and Phase II applications combined. The primary focus area will be used for determining limitations on institutional submissions. This announcement provides only opportunity highlights. Please carefully review the program guidelines and the sponsor's website for any requirements and expectations related to eligibility, PI and Co-PI limits, budget, collaboration, priority research areas, letter of intent requirements, and review criteria before submitting to this internal competition.
