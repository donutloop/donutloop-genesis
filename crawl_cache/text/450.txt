Project teams will design and demonstrate research workflows that integrate AI with scientific investigation, while rigorously evaluating whether those approaches can accelerate discovery, improve predictive capabilities, enhance experimentation, or generate new scientific insights. Jefferson Lab is a partner on these projects: AI-Driven Expedited Petabyte-Scale Streaming Readout The U.S. Department of Energy is developing the Electron-Ion Collider (EIC), a next-generation nuclear physics facility that will generate continuous detector data streams approaching 100 gigabits per second. At these rates, the fundamental challenge is no longer data collection, but real-time identification of rare physics signals embedded within overwhelming background and pileup. The vision of this project is to redefine scientific discovery in high-rate experimental environments by embedding intelligent, physics-aware AI directly into the data stream. AI-Enabled Digital Twin for Injector Optimization and Control This project will develop an AI-enabled digital twin for particle accelerator injector systems to improve how these systems are monitored, tuned, and operated. The work is led by Old Dominion University (ODU) in partnership with Jefferson Lab (JLab) and Pacific Northwest National Laboratory (PNNL) and is aligned with DOE Genesis Mission Focus Area 13A on AI-driven accelerator facilities. AI Computing Infrastructure for Collider Experiments Collider experiments at the High-Luminosity Large Hadron Collider and the Electron-Ion Collider will generate data volumes, rates, and processing complexity beyond what today's rule-based computing infrastructure can sustain. The MOSAIC (Multi-experiment Open Science AI Computing infrastructure) project is led by a highly experienced team at DOE's Brookhaven National Laboratory, DOE's Thomas Jefferson National Accelerator Facility (Jefferson Lab), the University of Texas at Arlington, and the University of Pittsburgh, with Google as collaborator. The aim of the project is to rebuild computing infrastructure around foundation models and AI agents, from detector data acquisition to physics analysis. Foundation Models for Reconstruction and Interpretation of Particle Interactions Future nuclear and particle physics experiments require artificial intelligence that moves beyond isolated detector- or task-specific tools toward integrated event-level modeling directly from low-level detector response. This project will establish a new class of scientific foundation models that unify detector response simulation, reconstruction, and interpretation within a common scalable framework. It will use the running GLUEX experiment at Jefferson Lab as a realistic Phase I demonstrator and will establish a pathway toward broader deployment in accelerator-based physics experiments. Real-Time Feedback and Neural Network Optimization of Polarized Targets In seeking to understand the structure of matter through nuclear physics scattering experiments, polarized targets are essential for probing nucleon spin structure. However, these systems rely on time-consuming manual methods for operation and analysis. Advances in Artificial Intelligence (AI) offer an opportunity to enable real-time optimization and faster analysis. In this Phase I project, the team will develop a unified AI-driven framework for control and analysis of polarized targets. Digital Twins for Particle Accelerators This project addresses a central challenge in realizing AI-driven accelerator facilities: how to create AI systems that are both powerful and trustworthy in complex, high-consequence scientific environments. This project aims to develop AI-enabled digital twins for particle accelerators: intelligent virtual representations of real machines that combine physics-based simulation with operational data to estimate hidden machine conditions, predict system behavior, and guide decisions under uncertainty. This proposal will focus on several accelerator systems at Brookhaven National Laboratory (BNL), the AGS Booster synchrotron, the Accelerator Test Facility (ATF), and the Electron Ion Collider (EIC). A Phase II effort would extend this capability to accelerator facilities at other DOE laboratories. Real-Time Integrity Protection of AI-Driven Scientific Workflows This project is a nine-month feasibility study led by Old Dominion University in collaboration with DOE's Thomas Jefferson National Accelerator Facility (Jefferson Lab). The vision is to establish a pioneering resilient scientific ecosystem where AI-driven discoveries are shielded from adversarial manipulation, ensuring that high-throughput facilities can maintain data integrity and operational safety even under sophisticated cyber threats. By integrating physics-informed guardrails, AI will enhance associated scientific and technical workflows by automating complex decision-making with a layer of "trusted intelligence" that distinguishes between rare physical phenomena and malicious interference.
